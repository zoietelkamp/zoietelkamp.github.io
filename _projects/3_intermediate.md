My current work spans intermediate scales (~1,000 au–0.1 parsec) to constrain the relative <b>dynamical importance of magnetic fields</b> in low-mass and massive star formation. Trends in protostellar outflow alignment provide key insights into the dynamical importance of magnetic fields in forming stars and circumstellar disks (e.g., <a href="https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4357/ad5a02">Green et al. 2025</a>, <a href="https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4357/ab07b9/meta">Kong et al. 2019</a>). Using both new and archival JWST NIRCam images, we're identifying outflows in both low-mass and massive star-forming regions and characterizing their orientations. We're assessing trends in the degree of alignment between these outflows and the core-scale magnetic field, inferred from archival dust polarization data, to investigate the relative strength and the role of magnetic fields in different environments and mass regimes.

As part of this project, I've developed a Python package with interactive tools to identify outflow structures and characterize outflow orientations and tested it on JWST NIRCam and ALMA data. I plan to release this package publicly on GitHub with GUI-based outflow analysis tools, documentation, and tutorials.
